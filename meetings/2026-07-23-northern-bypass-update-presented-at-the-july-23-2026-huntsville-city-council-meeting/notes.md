## TL;DR

- The Northern Bypass — the **State Route 255 extension, 4.5 miles of new
  limited-access highway from Pulaski Pike to ~1,000 ft east of US 231/431**
  (original cost **$43M**, replacing two-lane Bob Wade Lane) — is **~75% complete**;
  contractual completion is **November 2026**, and the city is pushing to have
  traffic moving both directions **by end of calendar year 2026** despite weather
  delays.
- Once open it completes a **14-mile limited-access loop from I-565 to US 231**
  (only signals: Mount Lebanon and Pulaski Pike); traffic modeling projects it cuts
  **~15 minutes** off north Huntsville/county commutes to Redstone Arsenal and pulls
  **~15,000 cars/day** out of the I-565/US 231 "malfunction junction" interchange.
- One engineering surprise: the mountain cut near Mount Lebanon turned out to be a
  **"mixed face" (part dirt, part rock)** rather than the solid rock the design
  assumed, so ALDOT will add slope stabilization (slope paving or riprap) — extra
  time, but outside the travel lanes and **not expected to delay opening**.
- **Phase 2 (US 231 → Winchester Road/Moores Mill Road** *(captions: "Winchester
  Mooresville" — verify)***) is at 60% plan completion and FHWA has now agreed to
  the shorter logical terminus** (the city argued ~a year against being forced to
  US 72, a ~$180M scope) — Davis believes it can be **bid next year**. Eastern
  bypass (Hampton Cove → US 72) corridor study is done; design money, approved in
  2025, starts being spent next year.
- **North Village Town Center** (200-acre mixed-use at US 231/SR 255): site
  utilities ~95% done, **Target and Home Depot real-estate closings Aug–Sept 2026**,
  vertical construction this fall, **phase 1 (~350,000 sq ft of retail) opens 2027
  and is 100% spoken for**; Huntsville Hospital breaks ground next year on a
  **$50M emergency & imaging center** in phase 2.

## Discussion highlights

**Project scope and status (Davis).** 4.5 miles of new curb-and-gutter,
limited-access four-lane divided highway with full utility relocation (water, gas,
electric, communications — all in place ~6 months except a bit of Huntsville
Utilities electric work near Toyota). The eastern end at US 231/431 was bid as an
early-completion requirement, so that intersection is finished and carrying heavy
traffic — already used as a morning alternate route to Research Park, Redstone
Arsenal, and the western corridor. Crews are now in the hardest section: hammering
and processing rock through the lower mountain slope near Mount Lebanon Road
(Cecil Ashburn-style work, slow by nature). Status ~75%; contractual calendar days
run to **November 2026**, with some weather days likely added (federal-aid
project), and both contractor and engineering being pushed to open the road **by
end of 2026**.

**Mixed-face slope issue (Davis, answering Watkins).** Geotech predicted solid
rock at the mountain toe that would self-stand as a bare rock face (like Governors
Drive / Cecil Ashburn). Instead it's a mixed face — part dirt, part rock — so a
supplemental stabilization plan (slope paving or riprap) is being worked; **the
state handles it since it's a state highway**. It adds time but sits outside the
traffic lanes, so it shouldn't hold up opening.

**Commute impact (Watkins's question).** Federally required traffic modeling
projects **~15 minutes saved** versus today's US 231 → I-565 westbound route for
northern residents heading to the Arsenal, plus **~15,000 cars/day diverted** from
the I-565/US 231 interchange (itself a ~$350M future project) — relief that
benefits south, downtown, east, and west Huntsville alike.

**Mount Lebanon intersection (Meredith's question).** It will be a conventional
signalized intersection with full turning movements on a four-lane divided highway
— comparable to Nance Road at US 72 — not a U-turn/service-road configuration.
Meredith also confirmed the SR 255 western terminus: **Rideout Road at the Arsenal
gates by Bridge Street**.

**Road naming.** The new highway gets SR 255 signage and — per Watkins's meetings
with neighbors — will locally keep the **Bob Wade Lane** name; the leftover
two-lane stub of old Bob Wade Lane must then be renamed. The **Sharp family**
(long-time residents on that road) indicated openness to lending their name — no
commitment made; the council will decide. Kling joked the eventual ring road
should be "the Kling Ring" *(captions: "cling ring")*; colleagues noted roads
can't be named for the living — "unless you put it in John Hunt Park."

**Phase 2 and the ring road (Kling's question).** This project is Northern Bypass
phase 1. Phase 2 (US 231 → Winchester Road/Moores Mill Road *(captions:
"Winchester Mooresville" — verify)*) is at **60% plan completion**. FHWA's
logical-termini rule pointed to US 72 as the next endpoint — a ~$180M scope — but
after ~a year of advocacy, Davis and City Engineer Kathy Martin *(captions:
"Cathy")* got confirmation ~two weeks prior from **Mark Bartlett of Federal
Highway** that the shorter phase can proceed; right-of-way is limited (large-tract
owners) and the city can fund it now. **Bid possible next year.** The **eastern
bypass** (Hampton Cove → US 72): corridor study complete, alignment known, US 72
tie-in sent to ALDOT and approved this year; design-plan funding was approved in
the 2025 capital plan and design starts next year.

**North Village Town Center phase 1 (bonus update).** 200-acre mixed-use
development at US 231/SR 255: 650,000+ sq ft of retail, restaurants, medical,
hospitality, and fitness plus new residential. City-side utility relocations, sewer
rerouting, burying a public drainage ditch (to maximize sales/property-tax
outparcels), and a regional detention pond along the TVA lines are ~95% complete.
**Real-estate closings with Target and Home Depot in August and September**;
tilt-up walls this fall; **phase 1 opens 2027** (full build-out ~3 years, 3
phases). Phase 1's ~350,000 sq ft of retail is entirely spoken for by "best in
class" household names (master developer announces end users after closing). Food
City anchors the south; one outparcel in front of it already sold *(captions:
"Abilene Express" — garbled, verify)*, with negotiations underway to sell three
more for sit-down restaurants, coming to council shortly. **Phase 2:** Huntsville
Hospital has closed on its parcel and is in full design for a **~$50M emergency
and imaging center breaking ground next year**. Phase 3 property is in final
negotiations. The Harris family land at 255/Mount Lebanon (item 9e tonight) and
the **Camden Place** single-family subdivision (under construction) round out the
corridor. Watkins: "absolutely proud and ecstatic."
