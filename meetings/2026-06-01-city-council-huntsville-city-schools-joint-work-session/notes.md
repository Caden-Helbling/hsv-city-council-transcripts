## TL;DR

- **This was a work session — no votes were taken.** Three presentations (growth/demographics,
  the HCS capital plan, and tax-increment financing) followed by discussion between the two bodies.
- **The proposed TIF District 9** was the centerpiece: ~3,700 acres of central Huntsville, five
  projects, an est. **14–15 year payoff**, and — per staff — **not a tax increase** and **no
  diversion of existing school/city/county revenue** (only new incremental growth is captured).
- **TIF 9's five projects:** Von Braun Center **North Hall expansion** (largest), the **North
  Huntsville Beltline Greenway** (first phase), a **$5M new Mill Creek Park** (Choice Neighborhood
  redevelopment with the Housing Authority), **Lowe Avenue + Huntsville Junior High improvements**
  (~$1M city road/sidewalks + ~$4M to the school board for the new junior high), and **$5M** to
  renovate the **former federal courthouse** for Madison County's courts/offices.
- **HCS board member Ms. Alvarez pushed back**: of ~$406M across the open + proposed TIFs, only
  **$5M** goes to schools, and the district's payoff is a generation out — she argued the schools
  feel present-day growth (kids "in classrooms that are formerly closets" at Providence) without
  a matching short-term windfall. Staff/board countered with the sales-tax gains and the donated
  District 5 school land as offsetting near-term city support.
- **The city's demographer** reported 2025 was the 2nd-highest year on record for new housing;
  single-family starts are rebounding (+40% YTD) as the multifamily boom cools. Top growth zones:
  **Providence** (dominant), Limestone/West Huntsville, North Huntsville (Rolling Hills/MLK), Hampton
  Cove, South Huntsville (Hays Farm).
- **HCS capital plan:** ~**$600M**, 10-year (started 2024), DOJ-court-approved; ~55–60% under
  contract; ~60–65% publicly financed off the **6.5 mills** the city collects for HCS.

## Discussion highlights

**Demographics (1a).** Vandiver: 2025 was the **2nd-highest year on record** for new housing (2024
was #1), but overall starts fell 24% as the historic multifamily boom cools; **single-family starts
rose 2% in 2025 and are up ~40% YTD in 2026**, with subdivision-lot approvals the highest since 2007.
Expect ~2,000 units this year (above the pre-2020 norm of 1,000–1,500). Modeling rules of thumb he
gave for school projections: single-family ~90% occupancy / ~2.5 persons per unit; multifamily ~80%
/ ~1.5–2.0. Meredith asked how last meeting's ~200-acre C-4→R-2 rezoning affects forecasts (Vandiver:
slightly *lowers* projected units). Board asked for this granular, per-zone data **annually**.

**HCS capital plan (1b).** ~**$600M+**, 10-year (2024 start), court/DOJ-approved Sept 2024; funded
~60–65% public financing (off the **6.5 mills** the city collects for HCS), ~20% local, ~20% state.
Now ~**55% under contract** (~60–65% once the Limestone County P-8 lets). Projects touch every feeder
pattern: new central office/CTE building (open ~1 yr), turf on all 10 ball fields, gym replacements,
Columbia HS Innovation Center + JROTC facility, Hampton Cove/Goldsmith classroom adds, a Jemison-McNair
6th-grade wing (open **Aug 2026**), Chapman & Montview STEM magnets (Montview open **Aug 2027**), a new
**Limestone County P-8** (groundbreaking ~fall 2026, open **Aug 2028**), and a future Hampton Cove site
(~2033). Board member noted enrollment is ~flat (~24,000, same as 10 yrs ago) but geographically
shifting, forcing **three school consolidations/closures**.

**TIF 9 — what it is (1c).** Davis framed TIF as capturing only *new* incremental property-tax value
inside a boundary to fund public improvements, after which the district closes and all proceeds flow to
the taxing bodies. History: since 2000 the city's TIFs have driven >$8.5B in private investment and now
generate ~$110M/yr once closed (~$47M/yr to HCS, ~$22M city, ~$20M county). **TIF 9** would be ~3,700
acres of central Huntsville, ~$29M 2025 baseline, projected **14–15 year payoff**. Its five projects:
- **VBC North Hall expansion** (largest) — the current hall turns away large defense conferences (e.g.
  the SMDC/"USA" show ran ~70 vendors short three years running; Quad-A left for Nashville).
- **North Huntsville Beltline Greenway** — first phase, modeled on the Atlanta BeltLine; runs the east
  side of the Parkway (Meridian/Church St) and is meant to **link downtown → Holmes Ave → UAH / Alabama
  A&M / Cummings Research Park → John Hunt Park**.
- **New Mill Creek Park** — **$5M** public park anchoring the Choice Neighborhood redevelopment with the
  Huntsville Housing Authority (vertical build is private, so it pays the full 58 mills).
- **Lowe Avenue + Huntsville Junior High improvements** — ~**$1M** city share for the road and
  **pedestrian/sidewalk facilities so students can walk to school**, plus ~**$4M** to the school board
  toward building the new Huntsville Junior High.
- **Former federal courthouse renovation** — **$5M** to ready the GSA-donated building for Madison
  County's courts/offices during their courthouse rebuild.

**The schools' pushback (Ms. Alvarez).** Of ~$406M across the two open TIFs plus TIF 9, only **$5M** is
allocated to schools; she asked why "education money isn't being invested into education" and pressed the
math — the net present value the schools forgo (~$6.1M over 14 yrs on TIF 9) roughly equals the $5M going
in, but the *new* growth inside the district is captured for the payoff period rather than reaching schools
during it. Davis and Mayor Battle answered with the long-game precedent (Mazda-Toyota/TIF 7: ~2,400 acres
taxed at ~$180K farm value would kick off ~$18–19K/yr; post-investment it produces ~$17M/yr once closed)
and board members added the **sales-tax gains are immediate**, plus the city's **donated District 5 school
land + funding** offsets the roughly break-even TIF 9 property-tax figure. Meredith and Kling probed the
7%/yr assessment cap and payoff mechanics; Davis confirmed a faster payoff is plausible (as with TIF 6,
which delivered HCS a ~$5.5M check on closure).

**Lowe Mill "tax island."** Davis flagged that **Lowe Mill sits inside the TIF 9 map as a carved-out
"tax island"** (the black box on the map) because it is **in the county, not city limits** — TIF work can
only occur within corporate limits. If Lowe Mill were annexed, any new revenue there would flow **directly
to the taxing bodies (not the TIF)**, which staff framed as a benefit/incentive to annex.
