## Discussion highlights

**TIF District 9 (9a — public hearing).** Urban & Economic Development director **Shane Davis**
presented the plan the council had ordered at its April 23 meeting. The district is ~**3,089 acres**
of central Huntsville (roughly north of Oakwood Ave to Drake Ave; California St east to the
565/14th-St "Stovehouse" area), authorizing **up to $220M** of qualifying public improvements. By
state law all five projects must begin within five years or their share drops off. Davis stressed —
and Kling walked through for the public — that a **TIF is not a tax increase**: millage and
assessments are unchanged; the district only captures *new* incremental tax value created inside the
boundary to repay the improvements, after which it dissolves and the new base flows to the city,
Madison County, and the schools. Modeling projects **$15–17M of net new annual tax base** at buildout.
The **five projects**: (1) **Von Braun Center North Hall expansion — ~$200M** (new exhibit/meeting
space, parking, a South Hall entrance/**access bridge** over part of Von Braun Dr — framed as keeping
big defense conventions like the annual USA symposium from outgrowing the venue); (2) **North
Huntsville Beltline Greenway — $5M**, a first phase modeled on Atlanta's BeltLine that **starts
downtown, connects to the ongoing Big Spring Park project, and runs north to Oakwood** with pocket
parks/greenspace; (3) **new Mill Creek Park — $5M**, part of the Choice Neighborhood redevelopment
with HUD and the Huntsville Housing Authority (encloses a drainage ditch into a park); (4) **Lowe
Avenue improvements — $5M**, roadway/pedestrian/drainage work supporting the new Huntsville Middle
School; and (5) **renovation of the former federal courthouse** (GSA-donated; possible temporary
Madison County Commission use). **Next steps:** notice to the two deferred tax recipients (HCS supt.
Dr. Clarence Sutton; County Commission chair Rex Bond), plan certified ~May 28, County Commission
votes first, then the council ~June 11, with the district active ~June 15.

**Two residents commented on the TIF.** *Joy Johnson* opposed the growth premise and pressed on
affordable housing and Mill Creek's long delays; *Darren Vraag* questioned why so much (~90%) goes to
the VBC rather than affordable housing or the transit hub.

**Council TIF questions — and the pedestrian/greenway details.** *Little* asked about **Lowe Mill**:
Davis confirmed it is **in the county and carved out of the district as a "tax island"** (a black box
inside the boundary) because TIF work must be inside corporate limits — if Lowe Mill later annexes,
its new value stays *outside* the frozen district. *Meredith* asked whether the **"sky bridge" in the
Pinhook Creek remediation** is in the TIF; Davis said **no — those are two separate federally-funded
grant projects, already under construction, unaffected by the TIF.** *Meredith* also questioned the
$5M courthouse spend and whether the County would reimburse (Davis: it's a city-owned asset needing
ADA/HVAC upgrades regardless; any County lease terms would come back for a separate vote). *Watkins*
and *Kling* framed the TIF as a city–county–schools partnership.

**Redstone "gift offer" for U.S. Space Command (20e).** Held for discussion after Joy Johnson
objected to city dollars going toward Army senior-leader housing. Davis explained it as a locally
funded **incentive** tied to U.S. Space Command's permanent relocation to Redstone Arsenal (stability
for the local economy); Kling asked where the money actually goes (federal government vs. an
individual's residence).

**New Huntsville Middle School.** Kling and Little attended the groundbreaking earlier that day; the
school is expected to open in ~2 years and ties into the TIF's Lowe Ave improvements.
