## Discussion highlights

**Public Safety Chaplains & Invocation**
Chaplain Rachel Harper offered the invocation, followed by the Pledge of Allegiance led by Councilmember John Meredith.

**Big Ed's Pizza Recognition (Item 6a)**
Councilmember David Little presented the resolution recognizing the 65-year legacy of Big Ed's Pizza. During the presentation, Little noted that Big Ed's was one of only five or six businesses in Huntsville to survive that long. Councilmember Robinson joked about the lack of pizza brought to the meeting, stating she would change her vote to "no" if no food was provided, but ultimately voted in favor of the recognition.

**Chaffee Community Volunteers (Item 6b)**
Councilmember Jennie Robinson presented the recognition for the Chaffee Neighborhood Civic Association volunteers. Speakers Lyle Boyles and Bill Gould detailed their 15-year partnership with the Parks Department to open/close Logan Park, monitor safety, and maintain facilities. They highlighted the reduction in vandalism and "nonsense" since the program began and praised the responsive nature of the Parks Department (specifically mentioning "David Della" as a key contact).

**Legal Services & Executive Session (Item 8a)**
City Attorney Trey Riley requested an executive session to discuss the legal ramifications of a new agreement with Riley & Jackson, P.C. regarding Fire Apparatus Antitrust Litigation. The Council voted unanimously (roll call requested) to enter executive session. Upon reconvening, the Council approved the authorization for the Mayor to enter the agreement.

**Nuisance Abatement & Demolition (Items 9a-9d)**
*   **4704 Cavett Dr (9a):** Approved for nuisance abatement.
*   **4204 Barry St (9b):** Approved for demolition. Councilmember Michelle Watkins noted a potential buyer for the property in September. Scott Irwin (Community Development) clarified that the nuisance abatement would proceed regardless of the sale, as the new owner would be held to the same standards, and demolition would occur 30+ days after mobilization.
*   **3042 Boswell Dr (9c):** Approved for demolition due to 90% loss and structural failure. Councilmember Little noted the photos were from May, but staff confirmed the condition had not improved.
*   **Cleanup Assessment (9d):** Approved an assessment of **$6,752.94** for cleanup costs at 3607 Princess St and 4906 Clarity Rd.

**Expenditures & Finance (Item 14a)**
The Finance Committee approved **$53,617,414.27** in expenditures. Director of Finance Penny Smith explained the large figure was driven by two major payments: **$14.4 million** for school system capital plan debt and the remaining balance covering the purchase of assets (food/beverage) from the Huntsville Venue Group. Without these two, the total would be approximately $27.7 million.

**Surplus Property Sale (Item 19a)**
Director Shane Davis presented the sale of 15 acres at Moores Mill Rd and Vintage Dr to Oppidan, Inc. for **$1,126,000** ($75,000/acre). The property, formerly part of the Qualitas Pharmaceutical campus, will be used for a regional fiber network switch facility. The agreement includes a repurchase option if the project fails and commits the buyer to building a new road access point for a future fire station (estimated 2-5 acres) at no cost to the city. Proceeds will fund the "1990 Plan" for this project.

**Surveillance & Flock Safety (Public Comments)**
Multiple speakers (Christopher Boulware, Maggie Minsk, Austin Jones, Kayden Helbling, Molly Cluck, Edward Ponytosky, Tyler Gottney) spoke against the city's use of Flock Safety ALPR cameras, citing privacy concerns, security vulnerabilities, lack of public oversight, and the risk of foreign adversaries mapping defense personnel. They requested the cancellation of the contract and a local ordinance requiring warrants.
*   **Council Response:** Councilmember John Meredith noted a forum on Flock cameras would be held on September 16. No immediate action was taken to cancel the contract, though the debate was a significant portion of the public comment period.
