## TL;DR

- **The Huntsville Hospital → Crestwood acquisition dominated the night.** CEO Jeff Samms gave a
  long presentation defending the $450M deal (local governance, reinvestment, not a monopoly,
  no rate-setting power). It got heated: **Watkins accused the hospital of a transparency
  failure and a snub of District 1 board representation**, a testy exchange with Robinson led to
  a recess, and multiple public commenters called the deal an illegal monopoly.
- **TIF 9 formally launched.** Council set the May 14 public hearing and authorized Urban
  Development to prepare a project plan for a **~3,689.58-acre downtown/central-city district**
  (Res 26-349, 26-359, 26-360). This is the same TIF later adopted July 9.
- **Four Boxwood Dr NW houses (1302/1304/1306/1308) approved for demolition** — all 100% loss,
  one used as a homeless encampment; owners given ~60 days (effective June 30) to self-demolish.
- **$33,866,708.71 in expenditures approved.**
- **59.21 ac at N Memorial Pkwy/Bob Wade Ln rezoned to C-4** — the future **Huntsville Hospital
  north-side** site.
- **Meredith abstained** on the Premise Health/BCBS confidentiality agreement (20T); everything
  else passed by voice vote. Item **10c (3804 Melody Rd) was withdrawn** by the sponsor.
- Long second-roster session (12 signed up): ICE non-cooperation, Elbit Systems/Gaza, hospital
  staffing/pay, and district-funding transparency were recurring themes.

## Discussion highlights

**Huntsville Hospital / Crestwood acquisition (7a).** CEO **Jeff Samms** presented on the
$450M purchase of Crestwood from Community Health Systems. Core claims: the deal keeps
governance and cash local (previously >90% of Crestwood's cash left the community to
shareholders); $4M already committed to Crestwood in 90 days (4× its recent annual capital); no
executive comp is tied to the deal; the system does **not** set prices (Medicare/Medicaid pay
below cost; one commercial insurer holds ~90% of the Alabama market) and cannot dictate nurse
pay (new-grad inpatient rate rising to $29/hr in July, gross avg ~$45/hr; a ~$30M nursing
comp increase in July). Crestwood keeps its name and staff. **Kling** asked about nurse pay,
branding, and physician privileges; **Meredith** drilled into the $450M price, blood-bank
upgrades, EMS training levels, and whether scale lets the system negotiate better rates (Samms:
system-wide negotiation, but no rate-setting). **Watkins** delivered a pointed critique —
constituents' questions went unanswered for months ("acquisition in place, can't discuss"),
which she called a transparency failure, and pressed hard for a **District 1 resident** (not
merely someone who works there) on the hospital's 11-member board. The exchange escalated with
Robinson over interruptions/decorum; Meredith moved for a **5-minute recess**. On reconvening,
Watkins and Robinson each asked further governance questions (interstate oversight, Crestwood's
local board continuing under Mike Gillespie).

**TIF 9 (20c/20d, with 10a).** Urban & Economic Development director **Shane Davis** walked
council through the three statutory steps to create a TIF: set a hearing (done, May 14), authorize
the President/Pro Tem to handle notices, and direct Urban Development to prepare a project plan.
The proposed district is **~3,689.58 acres** across the central business district (roughly
Bob Wallace/Drake to north of Holmes Ave), carved to exclude **Lowe Mill**, which is a
"tax island" still in the county (not in corporate limits). Davis stressed TIFs capture only
*future* assessed-value growth (existing taxes keep flowing to schools/county/city), and said
in 25 years he can't recall a Huntsville TIF that didn't pay off early. TIFs can overlap and are
unlimited in number.

**Averitt Properties surplus sale (19e).** Davis: the city sells a 4-acre sliver of its 2018
Eli Lilly/exit-3 land to Averitt Express (at appraised $60K/ac = $240,000) to connect two
Averitt parcels into an L-shape, letting ~53-ft trailers move internally instead of making
repeated turns through the busy Alabama-20/Greenbrier intersection — a safety win near ~14,000
double-shift jobs in that corridor.

**Old Monrovia Rd complete street (20o).** Davis described a **road diet** converting the old
6-lane mall frontage (former Madison Square Mall) into a slower, walkable "complete street" from
University Dr into Research Park East / Mid-City, with multi-use paths, on-street parallel
parking, lighting and landscaping; ~6-month build, first phase of a longer downtown-to-UAH
linkage.

**Public Safety Facility renovation (20q).** General Services' Ricky Wilkinson: **$3,177,300**
to convert the former **Bailey Cove library** into HPD office space (vacating leased space).
Little confirmed this is **not** the future South police precinct (insufficient parking/space).

**Intercity passenger rail study (20ab).** Planning's Dennis Madsen: a **$348,874.55**
MPO-funded study (DB ECO / Deutsche Bahn) to identify viable city-to-city ("high-efficiency,"
not high-speed/light) rail corridors and grant sources, and to get Huntsville — absent from the
2021 national rail plan — "a seat at the table." Peers Chattanooga and Birmingham recently began
similar Atlanta-linking studies. Not related to the local Railroad Authority or the Depot.
