## TL;DR

- **No votes taken** — the sole agenda item was soliciting councilmembers' input to
  the Finance Department on the proposed FY 2027 budget; the whole session ran ~31
  minutes.
- **FY26 position (Finance Dir. Smith): on budget but flat** — no large revenue
  growth; the mid-year revenue reduction was driven mostly by a **Huntsville
  Utilities PILOT reduction**, partly offset by rising property taxes. FY27 revenue
  forecast starts after one more month of actuals.
- **Budget calendar set:** introduce at the **September 10** meeting, **special
  session Friday September 18**, adoption vote **September 24**.
- Recurring themes across members: **park upgrades and maintenance funding,
  sidewalks, residential street sweeping, public safety staffing, and better
  management of road-resurfacing contractors** (FY25 projects still unfinished in
  at least two districts).
- **Downtown library:** Kling and Robinson want the library board/director to state
  what they want (renovate, rebuild, or relocate) before the city acts; employee
  salaries are the board's call from the city's direct allocation — the largest the
  city makes to any outside agency.
- Meredith's one priced ask: **~$192,000 for full staffing of ladder trucks and
  hazmat units**.

## Discussion highlights

**FY26 fiscal check-in (Little → Finance Dir. Smith).** Little opened by asking how
the city is positioned at fiscal-year end. Smith: actuals are on budget but not ahead
of it; revenue was reduced at the mid-year revision, largely due to the **PILOT
reduction** (still being worked through with Huntsville Utilities), offset by
property-tax growth. She has done no FY27 revenue forecasting yet — waiting one more
month of actuals — but expects no large revenue growth.

**Little (District 2 area asks).** California Street Park facelift/pickleball —
Davis: the TIF assistance for the new Huntsville Middle School approved **the night
before (July 9)** opens the door; once the school (just bid, breaking ground soon) is
about a year into construction, expect **master-plan visioning sessions for
California Park** with the school system; John Hamilton closely involved. Kling
*(rendered "Mr. Clean"/"Mr. Clay" in captions — the July 23 bypass captions confirm
"Mr. Clean" is how they garble Kling)* noted residents
already improvise pickleball on the tennis courts and the park remains well
maintained. Jones Valley Park pond / greenway walking path near the Lendon
development *(captions: "Lyndon")* off Carl T. Jones Dr: half paved, half gravel —
Davis said **engineering can handle paving it now from greenway maintenance funds, no
budget item needed**, and he'd set that in motion that day. Also: keep public safety
and the road budget fully funded.

**Watkins (District 1).** Requested funding to refurbish and revitalize three
long-declining District 1 parks — **Cavalry Hill, Stoner Field, and Lakewood**
*(captions garble these as "Cary Hills/Caver Hills", "Stonyfield"; normalized to
known District 1 facilities — verify in minutes)*: upgraded athletic fields, improved
field houses. She allowed that if Lakewood's users have largely moved to the
**Richard Showers Center** (getting its own facelift), Lakewood could come off the
list. Later in the session she added: a **street sweeper more often in District 1**
— quarry and asphalt-plant truck traffic constantly drops rock at Winchester &
Pulaski and Mastin Lake & Pulaski on freshly paved streets (kudos to Vulcan for
sweeping its own frontage daily), and rocks embedding in pavement cause potholes.
Also asked for **replacement of a couple of aging fire stations** in the budget.

**Kling (District 4).** Roads: supports accelerating the **northern bypass** (with
Watkins), the eastern bypass toward Hampton Cove off US 72 East (with Little), and
the **East Arsenal connector** (with Robinson) — the "ring road" relieving stop-and-go
congestion — plus the **Parkway/I-565 interchange**; wants these first in line for
any state/federal windfall. Parks: bare-dirt areas at **Archer Park and Westlawn
Park** (the grass-seed approach that finally worked at Hastings Park). Continue
accelerating **residential street sweeping** — buy more equipment if landscape
management needs it. **Crosswalk on Triana Blvd at Fantasy Playhouse** *(captions:
"Trina Boulevard")* — possibly this fiscal year, else fund it in FY27. Keep the
**council-directed projects process**. Police/fire manpower plus **community
development inspectors** as the city grows outward. **Downtown library:** the city
has helped build new North and South Huntsville libraries; downtown "could use
something done to it," but he blames library management for basics (broken water
fountains) and wants to **hear from the library director and board** — renovate,
rebuild on site, or relocate, and how to keep operating during construction — rather
than the council forcing a plan. **Resurfacing:** loves it, but the gap between
milling/concrete work and asphalt leaves streets torn up for months; **FY25 road
projects are still incomplete** in his district and at least one other; ride herd on
contractors.

**Meredith (District 5).** Opened by crediting the administration's financial
commitment to District 5 and voicing support for Mayor Battle. Asks: **sidewalks on
Balch Road, Old Railroad Bed Road, and Zierdt Road** south from Martin Road to Barren
Fork *(captions: "Zert"/"Barn Fork" — verify)*, the last to serve the new **West
Huntsville Rec Center** at Martin & Zierdt; a pavilion at the **Fanning Preserve**
on Old Jim Williams Road (Hamilton working with Madison County Commissioner
Haraway); a **monument sign for the West Huntsville Rec Center** — the entrance
placement tempts dangerous premature left turns off fast-moving Martin Road; the
**dog park near the former Cabela's (now Bass Pro Shops)**; and the "infamous"
**second entrance for the west side of Providence**. New parks out west welcome —
but keep them economical: "grass and shade," no water features driving up build and
maintenance costs. Additional money for the **Children's Advocacy Center**. His one
priced ask: **~$192,000 for full staffing of ladder trucks and hazmat units**, so
outlying stations' ladder crews can perform search-and-rescue rather than standing
outside fighting fire. Also, pointedly: he has **never seen a street sweeper in
District 5** — "if we can get a park you can keep the street sweeper."

**Library funding mechanics (Robinson + Smith).** Prompted by recent public comments
about library raises: the city does **not** pay library salaries — it makes a single
direct allocation, and the library board and administration decide how to spend it.
Smith clarified: the city **owns the library buildings** in Huntsville; the
Huntsville-Madison County library system operates facilities in Huntsville, Madison,
and the county, segregating its Huntsville operating costs, which the city funds
(plus some state and county money). The allocation is among the largest the city
makes to any outside agency; **Smith will send council the allocation history**.
Robinson: salary decisions rest with the library board.

**Robinson (wrap-up).** Police and fire are **fully funded for staffing — the
challenge is recruiting**, not budget. The **Green Mountain fire truck** is moving
via private donations plus fleet allocations. Paving funding has roughly **doubled
in 10 years** and may have hit contractor capacity — the city is still catching up
on 2025 paving, and the bid process was changed to draw more contractors (confirmed
by Davis). Fund **parks and landscape maintenance** to keep pace with all the new
parks ("make sure we haven't outrun our headlights"). The **I-565 interchange** is a
state/federal funding hunt, increasingly crucial; the **Arsenal connector**
*(captions: "Starsal")*, mostly ALDOT-funded, will offload 565. Praised the
residential street-sweeper expansion. Looking ahead to the **new South police
precinct on the Parkway** — land acquired, wants it moving in the capital plan.

**Mayor Battle.** Many of the items raised are already in the budget or in the
works. Groups with significant capital needs — the library, EarlyWorks, the land
trust, and others — must fit within the **10-year capital plan**, typically entering
at year 10 and moving up as payments can be staggered responsibly.

**Closing (Smith).** Asked members to send their written wish lists to Finance so
staff notes match. **Budget schedule: introduce September 10 → special session
Friday September 18 → adoption September 24.**
